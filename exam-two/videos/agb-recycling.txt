How an aging star returns carbon

Two shells around a quiet core
After core helium runs out, a Sun-like star has a carbon and oxygen core surrounded by burning shells. Helium burns closer to the core, with hydrogen burning farther out. The huge, cool envelope makes this an asymptotic giant branch star. A cool surface does not mean a cool interior.

A shell can pulse
Hydrogen burning supplies fresh helium. Helium shell burning can become unstable, producing thermal pulses. The shells change their activity while the envelope expands and readjusts. This is different from the earlier helium flash that began helium burning in the core. The location of the burning matters as much as the fuel.

Make it, mix it, release it
Helium fusion makes carbon. Deep convection can carry processed material toward the surface, and strong winds carry it into space. Follow that chain: production, mixing, then loss. The lecture emphasizes these giant stars as a major source of the galaxy’s carbon. Their discarded material can become part of later stars and planets.
