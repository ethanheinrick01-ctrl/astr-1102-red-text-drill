Light follows curvature
A light ray can bend past a massive object and still escape. General relativity connects matter and energy with curved spacetime. The curved path changes the direction from which we see a background source; it does not require the source itself to move. The rubber sheet is an analogy, not a literal fabric pulled downward.

Several paths, one source
Watch light from one background galaxy take different paths around a foreground lens. An observer can receive several apparent images, including distorted arcs. The lens has redirected light, not created extra galaxies. A classroom photograph alone may not reveal the exact number of distinct background sources.

Different evidence
Near a black hole, plasma emits light while gravity redirects or captures it. A bright ring surrounds a shadow; it is not a solid surface shining through the horizon. A merger also changes spacetime and can send gravitational waves outward. Those waves are a different messenger from radio, optical light or gamma rays.
