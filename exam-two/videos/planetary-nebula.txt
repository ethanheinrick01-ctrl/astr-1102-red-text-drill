One dying star, two different objects

The envelope leaves; the core remains
As a Sun-like giant loses its envelope, a small, hot core is exposed. The expanding gas and the central remnant are different objects. The remnant becomes a white dwarf, roughly Earth-sized, which gradually cools and fades. A planetary nebula is the discarded gas, not a planet-forming disk.

Ultraviolet light makes the gas glow
Ultraviolet radiation from the hot central star ionizes the surrounding gas. That gas produces bright emission lines. But a telescope pointed at the central star also collects its stellar spectrum. So the spectrum depends on which region you observe. Move the observing window from the gas to the star and watch the contribution change.

A temporary, shaped shell
Planetary nebulae spread out and fade over tens of thousands of years. Rings, lobes, and viewing angle can make their shapes look very different. Companion stars can help shape the outflow; a particular shape alone does not prove a companion. For this course, follow the stated assumption that the Sun forms a planetary nebula.
