Filling the lobe
A star can expand until it fills its critical lobe. Gas then flows through the inner point region toward the companion. The donor loses this material; the receiving star gains it. These boundaries describe a rotating binary, so the effective force balance includes rotation as well as both stars’ gravity.

A curved stream
The transferred gas already carries orbital angular momentum. In this disk fed system it curves around the compact star, strikes the outer disk and gradually moves inward. The impact makes a bright spot away from the stellar center. Gravitational energy released by accretion can power light without restarting ordinary core fusion.

Two different clocks
The initially more massive star normally evolves first. Its lower mass companion can still be on the main sequence when the primary dies. If the binary stays bound, that companion may become a giant much later and feed the compact remnant. The primary’s death and the later donor expansion are separate events.
