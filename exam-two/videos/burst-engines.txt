Find the fading source
The prompt gamma ray flash can end quickly, but an afterglow may remain. Rapid follow up locates that fading counterpart and can connect it to a host galaxy. A secure redshift provides distance evidence. A nearly uniform distribution of burst directions, by itself, cannot tell us how far away the bursts are.

Two pathways
In the lecture model for many long bursts, a rapidly rotating massive star collapses and feeds a central engine through a disk. Jets emerge through the surrounding star. Many short bursts instead come from compact mergers. A neutron star merger with both gravitational waves and a gamma ray signal supports that second pathway. Duration alone is not a unique engine diagnosis.

A jet can miss us
Now keep the source and its distance fixed, but change the jet direction. A viewer inside the beam can see a bright prompt burst. An off axis viewer can miss it even though the explosion still occurs. Beaming makes the detected sample smaller than the full event population; it does not turn the engine off.
