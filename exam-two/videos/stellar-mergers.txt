Inside a common envelope
Unstable transfer can engulf two stellar cores in one gaseous envelope. Drag transfers orbital energy into that gas while the cores spiral closer. If the envelope escapes soon enough, a close binary can survive. If it remains, the continuing inspiral can merge the cores. The shared envelope does not guarantee one outcome.

A merger lights up
A merger can release orbital energy and produce a luminous red nova. In the observed example, an eclipsing binary’s period shortened before the outburst. That changing precursor supports the merger interpretation. Despite the similar name, a luminous red nova is different from hydrogen igniting on a white dwarf in a classical nova.

An apparent younger star
Mass gain or a merger can give a star more mass and mix fresh hydrogen toward its core. In an old cluster, the result may lie above and to the blue side of the ordinary turnoff: a blue straggler. Its evolutionary state looks younger, but elapsed time has not reversed. One such star does not reset the cluster’s age.
