Earth changes viewpoint
Annual parallax uses Earth's orbit as a change in viewpoint. Six months apart, Earth is on opposite sides of the Sun. A nearby star appears to shift against more distant stars. The star does not jump sideways in space.

Move the star farther away
Move the same star farther away, and its angular shift becomes smaller. The parallax angle is half the total six-month displacement.

Invert the relationship
With parallax in arcseconds, distance in parsecs equals one divided by parallax. A parallax of one tenth of an arcsecond means ten parsecs. Twice the distance gives half the parallax.
