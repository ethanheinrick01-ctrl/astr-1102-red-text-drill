Where the energy goes
In the lecture’s simplified collapse budget, about ninety nine percent of the energy escapes in neutrinos. About one percent remains for other channels. That remainder is not the same as visible light. Follow the escaping particles separately from the expanding, energized gas.

Why light persists
The first hot fireball follows shock heating. Later, radioactive nuclei deposit more energy as they decay. Nickel fifty six changes into cobalt fifty six, then iron fifty six. This decay chain can sustain the fading light; calling this process fission confuses two different nuclear mechanisms.
