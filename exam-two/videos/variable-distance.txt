A pulsating star becomes a distance marker

Watch the star and its light curve
A pulsating star changes its own structure and brightness. Watch its radius and light curve vary together. Not every variable works this way: an eclipsing binary dims because another star blocks light. The pulsation shown here is schematic.

Measure a complete period
Measure from one brightness maximum to the next equivalent maximum. That interval is the period. The height of the variation is its amplitude, a different quantity. Here, the luminous pulsator represents the class used in the next distance calibration step. Its period is measured in days.

Calibrate luminosity, then distance
Identify the variable’s type and apply its calibrated period luminosity relation. Within a given Cepheid calibration, longer periods indicate greater luminosity. Then compare intrinsic luminosity with received brightness, correcting for extinction. For equal luminosity, four times fainter means twice as far away.
