Spread the light
Luminosity is the energy a star emits each second. Apparent brightness is the energy arriving each second per square meter. Watch the same outgoing light spread across larger spheres.

Double the distance
Double the distance while keeping luminosity fixed. The sphere has four times the area, so a detector receives one quarter as much light. Triple the distance gives one ninth.

Reverse the inference
Equal apparent brightness does not mean equal luminosity. A star twice as far away needs four times the luminosity to look equally bright. These comparisons assume no intervening extinction.
