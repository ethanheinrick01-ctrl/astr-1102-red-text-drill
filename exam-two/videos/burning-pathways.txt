Proton–proton: hydrogen to helium
Start with the net result. The proton proton chain turns hydrogen into helium. The illustration combines four hydrogen nuclei into one helium nucleus, with energy and other products leaving. It summarizes the net reaction rather than every intermediate step.

CNO: catalysts, same net fuel
The C N O cycle also turns hydrogen into helium. Carbon, nitrogen, and oxygen participate in intermediate steps. The catalyst cycle returns to its starting nucleus while hydrogen has been consumed and helium released. Hotter cores favor this route more strongly.

Triple-alpha: helium to carbon
Triple alpha has a different fuel. Two helium nuclei briefly form beryllium; adding a third makes carbon. Some carbon captures another helium nucleus to make oxygen. In the lecture’s local approximations, triple alpha is more temperature sensitive than C N O, which is more sensitive than the proton proton chain.
